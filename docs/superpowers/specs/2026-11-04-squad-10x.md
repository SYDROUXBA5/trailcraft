# Squad demo 10X: design

Status: approved by Rémi on 2026-11-04. He asked "how can you make this 10X better?", saw the seven moves below and chose "Everything, A to G". This builds on the squad and command demo (docs/superpowers/specs/2026-11-04-squad-command-demo.md, build 2026-11-04a). It is still a demo for a chief of police, labelled "Demo — not for real operations".

## What 10X means here

Today the chief sees dots on a map. The 10X version is the only K9 command screen that shows where the scent is probably drifting, whether the perimeter is keeping up with the clock, and why this dog can be trusted. He leaves with a report he can forward. It all plays as one ten-minute story on one laptop, with no cloud. None of the police tools a market check looked at (TAK, Axon Respond, Motorola CommandCentral, Esri Field Maps, CalTopo) models scent or links a deployment to the dog's training record.

## Rules for every move

- **Pretend first.** Every move must work fully in "Try it with pretend officers" (one device, no account, no cloud). The real squad path comes second.
- **Cloud changes stay inside the squad collections.** New fields go into `squads/...` documents and the squad part of `firestore.rules` (still unpublished), kept in step with `test/fake-squad-rules.mjs`. **Never add a field to an existing synced document under `users/{uid}/...`.** The published rules may refuse unknown fields, which would break every handler's sync. Anything new about a dog (licence body, expiry) is stored on the device only for now.
- **Honest words.** Use "modelled guide", "rough guide", "draft record", "pretend". Never "secure", "evidence-grade", "sealed", "CJIS", "prediction" or "proof". Suggestions never move or shrink the perimeter; Command decides.
- **Nothing pretends to know the person's route in a real call.** Only pretend mode knows the hidden route, so no feature may read it except the end-of-scenario "where the pretend person walked" line.
- Everything that exists keeps working: solo use, runs, live share, team levels, Garmin import, contamination relay, the audit fixes and the CSP. The training run screen is the core product, so changes there need tests showing a normal run is unchanged.
- Rémi's Mac has Reduce Motion on. Motion added here must respect `prefers-reduced-motion` and show with `?motion=on`.

## The moves

### A. A believable demo, readable from across the room
- **Follow the dog.** During an incident the command map keeps the K9 team, the last known point (LKP) and the perimeter in view. Following pauses while someone pans or draws; a "Follow K9" button resumes it. Builds on `setLeadUnit`, `focusUnit` and `fitOnMap` in squad-ui.js and `fitIncident` in incident-ui.js.
- **Wall mode.** A Wall button for a TV or projector: large clock, the last five log lines and the unit list in big type down the side, short pin badges (RB, SEEN, LOST, RE) with details on tap or when zoomed in, and a night look for the satellite map (try the Standard Satellite night preset; if the imagery does not darken, a dim overlay).
- **Phone.** The incident panel shrinks to one slim strip ("K9-1 Rex · heading E · Re-found 1 min ago · 10 min") that opens on tap.
- **In-app sheets** replace the six browser `confirm()` boxes in incident-ui.js and squad-ui.js (End incident, remove a mark, remove a member, new code, leave, delete). Leave the rest of app.js alone.
- **Believable pretend squad.** Pretend walkers and the pretend dog stay out of buildings (walls.js `wallIndex` / `blockStep`). The pretend sergeant sets a wide perimeter early and tightens it later, as doctrine says, and stops reading the hidden scent path.
- **His department.** A small form before the pretend squad starts: department name and call signs replace "Demo squad" and the unit names.
- **Hear it.** The command screen speaks the K9 team's calls ("K9-1, lost it") through the existing coach voice. initIncidents does not pass a speech hook yet, so wire one in. It can be switched off.
- **Miles.** Distances follow the existing `settings.distUnits` setting (incident.js `metresWords` ignores it today). Do not add a second switch.
- **"Not heard from" line.** When a unit in an incident turns grey: "Foot 5: no update from the app for 2 min, check by radio".
- **"What's kept, and for how long".** One plain sheet built from the app's own constants (positions while On duty, deleted after 6 h; incidents after 7 days).

### B. Last-seen clock, a "how far could they be" ring, and a live log
- Command enters when the person was last seen (default: when the incident opened) and how they left (walking, running, vehicle). The incident form also takes a short handler brief: direction of travel, description, scent article yes/no.
- A large clock runs. A soft ring grows from the LKP at that pace, with a wider dashed ring for running. When the walking ring passes the drawn perimeter, the perimeter turns amber: "Perimeter now inside walking range". The panel shows "Dog on scent 9 min after last seen". Labelled "rough guide, not where the person is". Paces are editable. No upwind/downwind advice tint.
- A live incident log fills itself in plain words ("14:04 K9-1 Rex started trailing", "14:09 Lost it", "14:10 Foot 5 sighting"). Tapping a line moves the map and the replay slider to that moment. A removed point stays in the log, crossed out ("withdrawn by Sgt 1, 14:12"), instead of vanishing: in the cloud path a mark is soft-withdrawn, not deleted.
- Builds on incident.js `ring()`, `elapsedWords`, `clockWords`, `buildReplay` / `replayAt`, and incident-ui.js `everySecond` and `paintPins`. The cloud path adds last-seen, pace, brief and withdrawn fields to the incident and mark field lists in `firestore.rules`.

### C. Keep officers off the track
- **Downwind band.** When the LKP is set, a soft band from the LKP downwind, using the wind already fetched at the LKP (`incidentAirPoint`). Labelled "modelled guide from the forecast wind, not where the person went". It needs a new wedge function from a single point (geo.js `driftPolygon` needs a timed trail). No "start the dog here" pin.
- **Who walked the scene.** From the incident tracks between the incident opening and Start trailing, list units that came within about 30 m of the LKP on foot (cars left out by speed). Shown to the handler at Start trailing and on the command panel, for example "Before the dog: Foot 3 and 2A12 were at the LKP". Doctrine: tracks often lead to officers who walked the scene.
- **Foot officer inside the perimeter.** When a foot unit's position falls inside the drawn perimeter (leaving out the K9 team and its cover officer): one line to Command, "Foot 3 is inside the perimeter". Distances say "about"; GPS is 5–20 m off in town.
  - Only positions heard after the ring went up count. Heard in the last 2 minutes: the live line, spoken once if the voice is on, on the folded strip and in the log ("Foot 5 went inside the perimeter, about 270 m in"), so a moment that lasts seconds at ×10 is kept. Older, up to the 30 minutes the map still draws a position: a softer card line, "Foot 5 was inside the perimeter, last heard 6 min ago", never spoken.
  - Someone walking out of the ring, or to a rally point outside it, is left out only in the first minutes of the ring (the 2 minutes before it counts, and 1 more). After that they are listed in softer words, "Foot 5 is walking out of the perimeter, about 120 m in", because a track usually leads outward from the last known point and walking out along it lays scent over it. A rally point inside the ring excuses nobody; one outside it excuses those standing within 50 m of it. The pretend sergeant sets his rally point just outside the ring.
- **Cover officer.** Command can pick a cover unit with the K9 team. The panel shows the cover's distance from the handler, marked as possibly out of date.
- Builds on contam.js (contamination people already exist), incident.js `trackBuffer` / `joinTracks`. Worked out on the device; no extra cloud reads.

### D. The dog's record card
- When Command picks the K9 team, a card shows that dog's record from Trailcraft training: trails by age band, blind runs and how often the dog found the person on blind runs, wrong calls, last trained, how often the handler's "sure" calls were right, and honest gaps ("no blind runs over 4 h old"). A licence chip shows the licensing body and expiry as typed by the handler, amber within 30 days.
- The card is frozen into the incident when the dog is chosen. It shows the record; the supervisor decides. Wording never says the app justifies a deployment.
- Pretend mode: Rex and Bella get made-up records labelled pretend.
- Real path: the handler's own phone works the record out from its own sessions (debrief.js `debriefRates`, `ranBlind`, `varietyGaps`; store.js `dogStats`; call.js `calibration`). `findRate` covers all real-target runs, so the blind-run figure needs its own filter. The handler chooses "Share Rex's record with the squad", which writes a small bounded summary into their squad member document (squad rules updated, not published). Licence body and expiry are stored on the device only.
- A self-kept training log is not licensing (NPCC in the UK) or certification (state, POST, NAPWDA or USPCA in the US), and a court can ask for it. A short note says so where the card is shared.

### E. One-tap deployment report
- After End, "Deployment report" builds a PDF and a GPX file of the trail: map of the trail, perimeter and points; the log; the dog's record card; weather and scent conditions when the dog started; the delay from last seen to dog on scent; who walked the scene; units involved. Every page is stamped "Draft record, not an evidence system", and pretend reports "PRETEND DATA". No fingerprint or "check a report" screen.
- A 30–60 second replay video of the incident to forward, reusing clip.js.
- Builds on pdf.js `buildPdf`, app.js `reportMap` / `savePdf` (built for training trails, so the incident needs its own layout), share.js `toGpx`, native.js `shareFile`. The report is saved on the device, because cloud incidents expire after 7 days.

### F. The handler keeps the squad picture, and hears it
- When the handler taps Start trailing, the run screen keeps a slim squad layer: perimeter, sightings, roadblocks, rally points, the cover officer, and one line such as "Sighting 180 m ahead-left · cover 2A12 about 40 m behind".
- The phone speaks short squad lines in the coach voice ("Sighting, Foot 5, 200 metres ahead, left"); each kind can be switched off. The call buttons are big, dim red at night, with a few seconds to undo. The "How sure are you?" sheet is skipped during an incident.
- Pretend mode: a "Handler's view" button shows what K9-1's phone shows, driven by the same pretend incident; "Command view" goes back. No two-window sync.
- Never presented as a safety cue: positions can be 10–30 s old, and the website goes silent with the screen locked. The radio stays the main channel.
- A normal training run (no incident) must look and behave exactly as before; tests prove it.

### G. A scripted scenario
- "Start scenario" replaces today's pretend incident with a believable call-out on a story clock ("Burglary, suspect fled on foot, 02:14" or "Missing vulnerable person"), using the department name and call signs from A.
- Captions narrate each step in police order: dog called, ring growing, sergeant contains wide then tightens, an officer walks the scene and shows up in "who walked the scene", the dog loses the scent and finds it again, the person found inside the perimeter. A second script lets the person slip out through an uncovered side.
- At the end, "Show where the pretend person walked" draws the hidden route. It is shown as "where the pretend person walked", never as proof of the model or the dog.
- Speed stays in Rémi's hands; the demo label stays on every frame.

## Build order

1. Round 1: A and B (they edit the same paint and panel code).
2. Round 2: C and D.
3. Round 3: E and F (E needs B, C and D; F reuses A's map layers).
4. Round 4: G, then a whole-product check of the full demo.

Each round: build, two reviews (security and privacy; the chief's demo), skeptic check, fix, final check with tests, the single-file build and a headless walk-through with screenshots. The main session checks it, bumps the build and deploys.

## Not doing (and why)

- An officer-down button: it would only work with the app open; no push notifications yet.
- Live dog-collar dots: no public live feed from Garmin or Dogtra collars.
- Guessing the dog's behaviour from the handler's phone, a dog heat gauge, scores or histories for named officers, auto-shrinking perimeters, road-shaped reach maps, an Apple Watch app, voice marking.
